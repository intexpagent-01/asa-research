1. Executive summary

The challenge. Pacific small island developing states face some of the highest energy costs and lowest electrification rates in the world. Remote geography, small populations, extreme climate exposure, and thin institutional capacity make conventional grid extension uneconomic for most outer-island communities. An estimated 60–70% of rural households in Melanesia and 20–40% in Polynesia and Micronesia lack reliable electricity access.

What the evidence says. This brief synthesises 44 lessons from independently evaluated World Bank energy and rural electrification projects, including direct experience from Vanuatu, Philippines, Myanmar, Lao PDR, Indonesia, Bangladesh, Kenya, Somalia, Ghana, Rwanda, and Haiti. The evidence converges on six findings:

  1. Fee-for-service delivery through local institutions outperforms capital-grant models for off-grid solar sustainability.
  2. Hybrid technology strategies (grid + mini-grid + off-grid) are more resilient than single-technology approaches, but require clear coordination.
  3. Affordability is the binding constraint, not technology availability. Pre-project affordability surveys are essential.
  4. Grid expansion can overtake off-grid investment faster than expected, stranding assets. Design for integration, not isolation.
  5. Policy conditionality without political will blocks infrastructure delivery. Separate physical investment from institutional reform tracks.
  6. Project design simplification based on past experience produces better outcomes than complex multi-component designs.

Vanuatu’s direct experience. The Vanuatu Rural Electrification Project Stage II was rated Unsatisfactory by the World Bank’s Independent Evaluation Group — one of only three failures in the East Asia & Pacific energy evidence base. Its lessons are incorporated throughout this brief as direct warnings about what not to repeat.

12. Implementation checklist

Evidence-based actions to address before procurement. Each item is derived from a lesson in the evidence base — the consequence of not doing it is documented in at least one evaluated project.

  • Conduct household energy use and affordability survey in target communities
  • Map existing grid expansion plans and timeline for the project area
  • Assess regulatory environment: metering standards, tariff-setting authority, land use rules
  • Identify and assess capacity of local implementing institutions (utilities, cooperatives, CSOs)
  • Design hybrid technology strategy with grid, mini-grid, and off-grid components
  • Develop fee-for-service delivery model with prepaid metering for off-grid systems
  • Establish community engagement and land access framework before procurement
  • Build climate resilience standards into all infrastructure specifications
  • Design tiered subsidy mechanism: capital cost reduction + maintenance cost support
  • Train local technicians and establish spare parts supply chain for each technology type
  • Set outcome indicators that measure access, reliability, and affordability — not just connections
  • Establish donor coordination mechanism if multiple development partners are active
  • Include independent verification agent from project start, not mid-implementation
  • Design productive use of energy (PUE) component to support economic returns
  • Plan for grid integration pathway: off-grid systems that can connect when grid arrives

14. Sources and limitations

Source. World Bank Implementation Completion Report Reviews, accessed via the Documents & Reports API. 3,043 lessons from 991 projects were screened; 44 met the relevance criteria for this brief.

Method. Lessons were filtered by sector keywords (energy, electrification, solar, off-grid, mini-grid, renewable) and by geography (East Asia & Pacific, plus all rural/off-grid energy projects globally). Thematic classification uses keyword matching. Design principles are drawn from projects rated satisfactory or above; pitfalls from projects rated unsatisfactory or below; implementation guidance from moderately-rated projects.

Limitations.

  • This brief reflects what World Bank evaluators identified, which is one institutional perspective. Bilateral donors (DFAT, MFAT, JICA, ADB) have separate evaluation evidence not included here.
  • The corpus covers 991 of 7,300+ available ICR Reviews. Coverage of Pacific-specific projects is thin (2 direct lessons); the brief draws heavily on transferable global evidence.
  • Lessons are pattern-extracted from evaluation reports, not manually curated by sector specialists.
  • The evidence is retrospective (what happened) not predictive (what will work). Local context — particularly the extreme logistical constraints and thin institutional capacity of Pacific small island states — always dominates global patterns.
  • No model calls or AI generation are used in the analysis or synthesis. All content is deterministic extraction and keyword-based classification from the source documents.
