Cross-Domain Synthesis · September 2026
The Measurement Layer: A Cross-Domain Pattern
Across three independent data systems — aid transparency, governance indicators, and poverty statistics — the same structural pattern: the measurement methodology shapes the answer as much as the underlying reality. This is not a finding about data quality. It is a finding about the structure of development measurement.

The Measurement Layer (IATI)
What five analyses of IATI data reveal about the gap between questions and answers. The number you get depends less on what is happening on the ground than on how you define, count, and filter your data.
Analysis 1 · September 2026
What IATI Data Tells Us About Aid Fragmentation — And What It Doesn’t
A multi-layer decomposition of fragmentation metrics across 12 country-sector pairs in East Africa. Roughly one-fifth of apparent fragmentation is measurement artifact; the remainder conflates distinct coordination challenges.
Analysis 2 · September 2026
Is Aid Fragmentation Growing? What Fifteen Years of IATI Data Can and Can’t Tell Us
Tracking organizational entry and exit across ten country-sector pairs from 2010 to 2024. Every pair shows growth; none shows a sustained decline. But IATI’s expanding reporter base means the data cannot distinguish real growth from increasing visibility.
Analysis 3 · September 2026
The Coordination Blind Spot: What IATI Data Can and Can’t See About Aid Coordination
IATI has structured fields for coordination — basket fund codes, multi-funder records, participating org roles. Across 4,756 activities, the multi-funder field was used zero times. The “Accountable” role is filled for 28% of activities. 16% of coordination signals are invisible to structured queries.
Analysis 4 · September 2026
Testing the Reporting Expansion Hypothesis
IATI’s publisher base grew 2,051% from 2012 to 2024. Using publisher first-publish dates, we test whether apparent fragmentation growth is real or a reporting artifact. Result: roughly half of organizations entering each country-sector after 2015 were new IATI publishers.
Analysis 5 · September 2026
Counting Climate Finance: How Three Measurement Methods Produce Three Different Answers
Sector codes, keyword searches, and Rio markers identify almost entirely different pools of climate activities — just 7% overlap. The largest environmental donor (USAID) has zero marker fill across ~300 activities per country. The measurement method determines which donors’ activities count.
New: Governance Indicators
Analysis · September 2026
The Precision Illusion: What Governance Indicators’ Own Error Bars Reveal
The World Bank’s Worldwide Governance Indicators assign each of 206 countries a score on six governance dimensions. Taking the WGI’s own confidence intervals seriously: 43% of country pairs have overlapping CIs. Only 17.5% of governance changes over 17 years are statistically detectable. India, ranked 70th, could plausibly be anywhere from 31st to 132nd.
New: Poverty Measurement
Analysis · September 2026
The Poverty Line Paradox: How Five Methodological Choices Shape Who Counts as Poor
Same country, same year, same survey: the number of “poor” Indians ranges from 4 million to 969 million depending on the poverty line and PPP vintage — a 240× range. 75% of annual poverty data points are modeled, not measured. 26 survey comparability breaks across 10 countries fracture trend lines. The third domain confirming the measurement layer pattern.
