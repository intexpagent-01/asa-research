Strategic Context

Brisbane 2032 and sport diplomacy

PASC sits within Australia’s broader Pacific sport diplomacy strategy. In February 2026 the Australian Government announced AUD $15.6M over four years for elite athlete pathways toward Brisbane 2032. The PacificAus Sports program (AU$52M, elite sport) runs in parallel. PASC is the community/development arm — complementary but distinct in purpose and delivery model.

Combined Australian investment in Pacific sport now exceeds AUD $25M annually. The Brisbane 2032 Olympic and Paralympic Games elevate Pacific sport as a diplomatic priority through 2032 and beyond.

Geopolitical dimension

Sport diplomacy is explicitly framed as a Pacific engagement tool. Australia’s rugby league investment in PNG alone reached AUD $600M. PASC operates in this strategic context — it is a development program, but its diplomatic rationale strengthens the case for continued and potentially increased investment.

Program evolution

  • Pacific Sports Partnerships (PSP): AUD $39M total. Managed by Australian Sports Commission, then GHD from 2015.
  • Team Up (ASPP): 10-year investment at AUD $6M/year ODA. Sep 2018 – Sep 2028 (three contract phases). 35 partnerships, 15 sports, 70+ partners across 7 countries.
  • PASC: 10-year successor, expanding from 7 to 12 countries, integrating lessons from the OPM mid-term review (April 2024).

Incumbent Analysis: GHD

Strengths

  • 11 years continuous delivery. Managed Australia’s sport-for-development program since PSP in 2015, building deep institutional knowledge and relationships across seven Pacific nations.
  • “Outstanding value for money.” OPM’s April 2024 mid-term review rated Team Up highly across all 5Es (Economy, Efficiency, Effectiveness, Equity, Ethics). “Team Up is an extraordinary program and can be even better.”
  • Efficient resource use. 61% of funds reach sport partnerships on the ground. Supports 35 partnerships (vs 20 originally budgeted) — stretching resources effectively.
  • Strong Pacific team. Staff based in Suva and Port Moresby with Canberra support. Many team members are Pasifika with deep local context understanding.
  • Global recognition. Team Up helped DFAT become a “recognised global leader in sports for development program and practice” (MTR).
  • Strategic partnerships. Women Win, IOC/Olympism365, ABC International Development, ChildFund, UTS, sportanddev.

Vulnerabilities

  • 19 MTR recommendations. While the overall finding was positive, 19 specific improvement recommendations indicate scope for a fresher approach.
  • Team stretched thin. The MTR explicitly flagged that the management team is “quite stretched” with 35 partnerships vs 20 budgeted. PASC adds 5 new countries — a 71% geographic expansion.
  • No sustainability strategy. Recommendation 7 calls for one — standard DFAT practice at the program halfway mark, not yet produced after 8 years.
  • Government engagement weak. Rec 13: better engagement with Pacific national governments (sports, women’s affairs, education, health ministries) — a significant gap.
  • Inclusion strategy needed. Rec 1: strategy for inclusion of people of all abilities — programming for inclusion at scale was identified as a challenge.
  • GBV referral pathway risk. Rec 2: GBV awareness-raising without aligned referral pathways poses a significant risk.
  • ABC ID contracting issue. Rec 17 (flagged as urgent): ABC ID should be contracted through DFAT directly, not GHD — signaling friction in the managing contractor relationship.

Mid-Term Review Intelligence

Oxford Policy Management, April 2024. 19 recommendations.

Overall: “Team Up is certainly ‘scoring goals’ in the Pacific and beyond. Team Up is an extraordinary program and can be even better.”

The 19 Recommendations (grouped)

Inclusion and safeguarding (Recs 1–3): Build disability inclusion strategy; align GBV programs with referral pathways; reinforce safeguarding training.

Partnerships (Recs 4–6): Strengthen partnership brokering and due diligence; develop community of practice; support Pacific lead partners.

Sustainability and management (Recs 7–8): Prepare sustainability strategy; intentionally manage DFAT–GHD partnership.

Opportunities (Rec 9): Accept IOC/Olympism365 co-funding offer (up to AUD $1M/year additional).

MEL and reporting (Recs 10–11): Present MERL tools to DFAT for discussion; close the reporting circle to contributors.

Program continuity (Rec 12): Start extension process to September 2028.

Engagement and coherence (Recs 13–15): Government engagement strategy; join up with other DFAT programs; share resources with PacAus Sports.

Structural (Recs 16–18): Consider merging PacAus Sports and Team Up; re-contract ABC ID through DFAT directly; identify Pacific implementation partners for lead delivery.

What the MTR means for bidders

  • The overwhelmingly positive review (“outstanding value for money”) is GHD’s strongest card. Challengers cannot credibly argue the program is failing.
  • But 19 recommendations is substantial. Each one is an opportunity to say “we would do this differently.”
  • Three most exploitable gaps: (1) no sustainability strategy after 8 years, (2) weak government engagement, (3) inclusion strategy not yet developed.
  • The transition argument cuts both ways. For GHD: continuity is worth protecting. For a challenger: PASC is a new program (expanded geography, revised design) — the natural break point for a new approach.

Key Design Questions

  1. Geographic expansion model. Adding 5 countries (FSM, Kiribati, Palau, Marshall Islands, Tuvalu) — among the smallest and most remote Pacific nations. How does a managing contractor establish partnerships where national sporting federations may be minimal?
  2. PacAus Sports integration. MTR Rec 16 suggested merging the programs. PASC’s name explicitly positions it as the community complement to PacAus Sports. How integrated is the procurement?
  3. GBV and NCD outcome measurement. The MTR noted partners should be “realistic about the scale and sustainability of behavioural change outcomes.” How will PASC’s results framework handle contested evidence?
  4. Localisation trajectory. MTR Rec 18 calls for Pacific implementation partners. PASC’s 10-year timeframe creates a natural progression — how much Pacific-led delivery from Year 1 vs Year 10?
  5. ABC ID contracting. If DFAT follows Rec 17 (contract ABC ID directly), this removes a significant partnership from the managing contractor’s scope.

Positioning Recommendations

For a challenger unseating GHD

  1. Don’t attack the program — attack the transition. “Team Up was excellent. PASC is a different program — expanded geography, new design, new policy context. A new program deserves purpose-built delivery, not an inherited approach.”
  2. Lead with the five new countries. GHD has no presence in FSM, Kiribati, Palau, Marshall Islands, or Tuvalu. Every bidder starts equal there.
  3. Offer the sustainability strategy on Day 1. Include a detailed framework in the proposal — it’s a concrete deliverable GHD hasn’t produced after 8 years.
  4. Address government engagement. Bring existing relationships with Pacific sports, education, or health ministries. The MTR flagged this as weak.
  5. Propose a disability inclusion partnership. Name a credible partner (International Paralympic Committee, CBM, Pacific Disability Forum) with a concrete plan.
  6. Brisbane 2032 integration. Show how community sport development connects to the elite pipeline and Australia’s highest diplomatic priority.

For GHD defending the position

  1. Lead with the MTR. “Outstanding value for money” from an independent review is the strongest evidence any incumbent can have.
  2. Show MTR progress. Document which of the 19 recommendations have been addressed since April 2024. Especially Recs 7, 9, and 13.
  3. Emphasize continuity risk. 35 partnerships across 70+ organisations in 7 countries built on personal trust. A new contractor needs 12–18 months to rebuild them.
  4. Demonstrate expansion capability. Show existing presence, local staff, and relationships in the 5 new countries.
  5. Quantify the co-investment leverage. DFAT post co-investment, volunteer labour, in-kind contributions, and the IOC co-funding multiplier — hard for a new contractor to replicate in Year 1.
