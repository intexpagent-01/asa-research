What this checks

Development donors publish explicit quality frameworks for evaluation documents. This tool checks against two major bilateral donor frameworks:

  • DFAT (Australia): Investment designs scored against 9 quality criteria. Evaluation plans and reports assessed against DMEL Standards 9 and 10. DFAT's own 2024–2025 Quality Review found 40% of evaluation plans did not meet adequate quality.
  • USAID (United States): Evaluation reports assessed against ADS 201 mandatory requirements — 13 structural checks covering required sections from abstract to annexes. USAID's 76-factor quality checklist identifies 15 critical factors for early drafts.

Both frameworks share a common insight: most quality failures are structural — missing required sections, not subtle quality issues. This tool checks for the structural presence of required elements using pattern matching, not AI judgment. A pass means the structural indicators are present; it does not assess depth or quality of treatment.

Limitations

  • Structural, not qualitative. The checker detects whether required elements appear to be present. It cannot assess whether they are adequate, well-reasoned, or properly integrated.
  • Pattern-based. It matches keywords and phrases. A document that uses unusual terminology for standard concepts may score lower than its quality warrants.
  • No context. It doesn't know what kind of investment is being evaluated, so it can't adjust expectations (e.g., a humanitarian response evaluation may legitimately lack some elements).
  • Not a substitute for review. Use this as a pre-submission sanity check, not as evidence of quality.
