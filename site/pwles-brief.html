Mid-Term Review Intelligence

Overall finding: “Fit-for-purpose, highly relevant, and effective approach to accelerating and consolidating regional gender equality efforts, distinguished by its Pacific-led, collaborative and adaptive design.”

Key recommendations (9 total; 7 agreed, 2 partially agreed):

  1. Improve coordination between PWL components — quarterly strategic convenings with SPC, PIFS, PWLES, Women’s Funds, DFAT, and Governance Board. This is a direct challenge to the enabling services contractor’s core function.
  2. Additional recommendations focused on MEL improvement, Pacific-led implementation, and Phase II design.

What this means for bidders:

  • A challenger can legitimately argue that coordination — the enabling services contractor’s primary job — needs improvement
  • Phase II’s expanded scope (PAVE + Help Desk) creates genuine transition risk that a fresh approach could address
  • The “Pacific-led” framing is essential — DFAT wants the contractor to enable Pacific leadership, not substitute for it

Predecessor Lessons

Pacific Women Shaping Pacific Development (2012–2021), AUD $320M total:

  • Six-year evaluation found strongest results in violence against women response and expanded survivor services
  • Key lesson that informed PWL design: need for Pacific ownership and leadership rather than donor-driven implementation
  • Cardno (now DT Global) managed the Support Unit
  • The transition from PWSPD to PWL was explicitly designed to shift power to Pacific institutions (SPC as core delivery partner)

Positioning Recommendations

For a challenger unseating the incumbent

  1. Lead with coordination. The MTR said this needs improvement. Show a specific coordination model (quarterly strategic convenings, clear escalation protocols, shared dashboards) that addresses the identified gap.
  2. Demonstrate Pacific relationships independently. DT Global’s Pacific relationships are the strongest incumbent advantage. A challenger needs pre-existing SPC, PIFS, and Pacific government relationships — not promises to build them.
  3. Lean into GBV expertise for PAVE. Phase II adds GBV prevention. Cowater’s SE Asia GBV Platform or specialist GBV experience could differentiate here.
  4. Offer fresh MEL thinking. The MTR partially agreed with some recommendations — there’s room for a new approach that goes beyond what’s currently in place.
  5. Don’t just promise “Pacific-led.” Show how your approach structurally embeds Pacific leadership — SPC secondments, Pacific advisory panels, country-level governance structures.

For the incumbent defending the position

  1. Address the MTR coordination finding directly. Show what you’ve already changed and what Phase II will do differently.
  2. Emphasize continuity risk. A new contractor inherits a 60-project portfolio across 14 countries. The transition cost and knowledge loss are real.
  3. Show Phase II readiness. Demonstrate BOP and PAVE expertise, not just continuation of Phase I capability.
  4. Leverage the platform. pacificwomen.org, the MEL framework, QTAG, and the stakeholder network are assets a new contractor would need 12+ months to rebuild.
