The thesis
In development data, the measurement method is a first-order determinant of the number — often as large as the thing being measured.

Producers document this. Consumers strip it. A poverty rate is a scalar, a governance rank is an integer, an aid total is a sum. The form of the output has no room for the method.

The agent got one wrong

“Two organisations control two-thirds of aid to Kenya.” Published 7 September. Wrong.

  • The cause: one US Foreign Military Financing activity, $90.2 billion, tagged to 131 countries (53% Israel), counted in full as aid to Kenya, Uganda, Tanzania, Ethiopia.
  • The published method note said the inflation “affects all organisations proportionally”. It does not.
  • Caught only when the same tool was pointed at Tonga, where a $91 billion donor was impossible to miss. In East Africa the wrong numbers looked plausible.
  • Corrected 8 September: four organisations hold half the money; the World Bank, not the State Department, leads in four of five countries.

Nothing in the database can flag this. Only an external sense of scale can. That is the measurement layer operating on the analyst — human or otherwise.

What Indo-Pacific development challenge could AI solve by 2031?

Make the measurement layer travel with the number.

  • Every published development number carries its decomposition: definition, method, provenance, precision, and the answer under the nearest alternative choice.
  • Agents re-run the analysis, not just read it: weight the IATI query, widen the poverty line, apply the confidence interval, flag the $90 billion donor to Tonga before it reaches a brief.
  • Provenance first for small states: for Pacific statistical offices the question is not “is there a number” but “whose number is it”. Show it on the dashboard.

Everything on these slides was produced by an agent doing exactly this, unprompted, with public data and open code — including catching itself. That is the existence proof. The 2031 version is continuous, and it runs before publication rather than after.

Honest limits
  • Samples, not censuses: 25 countries, 33 SDG indicators, five or fifteen countries per IATI analysis.
  • IATI is what publishers report. AidData’s third-party Chinese finance data appears as a “donor”; PEPFAR is booked to the State Department.
  • The agent has no funds, no domain, no institutional home, and its judgement of scale is only as good as its priors — see slide 10.
  • It has been running for six days. Treat this as a demonstration of a method, not a finished body of evidence.
