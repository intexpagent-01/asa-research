Evaluation Standards Compared

DFAT · USAID · FCDO · UNDP — what each donor requires in evaluation reports, and where they differ

Development evaluations increasingly serve multiple donors. A single programme evaluation may need to satisfy DFAT's DMEL Standards, align with USAID's ADS 201, meet FCDO's EQUALS2 framework, or follow UNDP's Quality Assessment System. Each framework has its own vocabulary, emphasis, and blind spots.

This page compares the structural quality requirements of four major development donors side by side, covering 27 evaluation dimensions. It is a reference for evaluators, programme teams, and quality reviewers working across donor boundaries.

What Makes Each Donor Unique

Every framework has requirements the others lack. Knowing these helps you avoid gaps when an evaluation must satisfy more than one donor.

DFAT (Australia)

  • Chain of evidence — Explicit requirement for a logical chain from questions through evidence to recommendations — the only donor to name this as a separate check.
  • Evidence strength — Evaluators must assess and identify the strength of evidence used, not just present it.
  • GEDSI integration — Gender Equality, Disability, and Social Inclusion as an integrated framework across all document types.
  • Collaborative approach — Evaluation plans must describe how stakeholders collaborate in the evaluation design.
  • Quality assurance — Plans must describe the quality assurance approach for the evaluation itself.

USAID (United States)

  • Abstract (≤250 words) — The only donor requiring a formal abstract in addition to the executive summary. Designed for the Development Experience Clearinghouse (DEC) catalogue.
  • Evaluation type classification — Reports must explicitly identify whether they are impact or performance evaluations — a distinction central to USAID's evaluation policy.
  • Conclusions separated from recommendations — Uniquely emphasises structural separation: conclusions interpret what was found; recommendations say what to do about it.

FCDO (United Kingdom)

  • Independence and conflicts of interest — The only bilateral donor explicitly checking for independence disclosure and conflict-of-interest statements.
  • Ethics and safeguarding combined — Unique pairing of ethical conduct with safeguarding (do-no-harm protection of evaluation participants).
  • OECD DAC criteria — Requires the six DAC criteria (relevance, coherence, effectiveness, efficiency, impact, sustainability) as an organising framework.

UNDP (United Nations)

  • Human rights-based approach (HRBA) — The only framework requiring explicit integration of human rights principles throughout the evaluation.
  • Disability inclusion — Standalone requirement for disability-inclusive evaluation — stronger than any bilateral framework.
  • Environmental sustainability and SES — Must address Social and Environmental Standards compliance, including climate considerations.
  • Data analysis procedures — The only donor requiring a standalone description of how data was analysed (not just collected).
  • Leaving no one behind — Cross-cutting principle that must be reflected in methodology, findings, and recommendations.

Practical Guidance for Multi-Donor Evaluations

Start with the most demanding framework

UNDP has the broadest cross-cutting requirements (HRBA, disability, environmental sustainability). Starting with UNDP and then verifying the others' structural requirements is more efficient than building up from a bilateral framework.

The universal core

All four donors require: executive summary, programme description, evaluation purpose and questions, methodology, limitations, findings, conclusions, and recommendations. Get these right and you satisfy the structural baseline everywhere.

Watch for vocabulary differences

DFAT says "GEDSI," FCDO says "GESI," UNDP says "GEWE," and USAID expects gender analysis within programme evaluations. Same concept, four acronyms. Your document's search term must match the donor reading it.

USAID's unique structural demands

Only USAID requires both an abstract (≤250 words) and an executive summary. Only USAID requires classifying the evaluation type (impact vs. performance). Miss either and the Development Experience Clearinghouse flags it.

FCDO's quality bar is measurable

EQUALS2 assesses 69 indicators with a numeric score. In 2023–24, 87% of FCDO evaluations passed on first review. This is the most quantified framework — your report either meets the threshold or it does not.

Don't forget the management response

DFAT and FCDO require management responses as part of or alongside the evaluation report. USAID and UNDP manage responses through separate systems (Evaluation Registry and Evaluation Resource Centre). If your ToR includes it, include it.
