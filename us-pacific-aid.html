US Aid Goes Dark in the Pacific

Between 2026-09-28 and 2026-09-30, US government aid disbursements to all 14 Pacific island countries dropped from $979,673 to exactly $0 in the IATI 90-day monitoring window — and have not returned. This page tracks that change using 41 consecutive daily snapshots from the Pacific Aid Signal.

What happened

The mechanics of disappearance

USAID’s last published Pacific disbursements were dated around 30 June 2026. For 20 days (8–28 September), those transactions sat inside the 90-day trailing window. On 30 September, they aged out. No new US transactions replaced them.

The transition was abrupt — not because funding was withdrawn overnight, but because a batch of final disbursements all shared similar dates. When the trailing window moved past them, every US data point vanished simultaneously.

This is a data-confirmed absence, not a data lag. DFAT and FCDO are also absent from the 90-day window, but because they publish slowly (DFAT: 479+ days stale). US agencies were publishing current data until mid-2026 — then stopped.

Caveats. IATI data publication lags actual funding — some US programs may have final disbursements not yet published. Compact of Free Association (COFA) funding may continue through channels not reported to IATI. The $979K in September was already small; the larger US Pacific presence was in longer-term programs now ending. Small amounts ($100–$500 in some countries) may represent data artifacts rather than active programs. Lifetime figures include the full COFA compact payments reported by the Department of the Interior, which are large relative to other aid flows.

Context

USAID was effectively shut down in 2025 — 83% of programs canceled, staff reduced from 10,000 to approximately 15. The consequences are documented across the sector: Nepal lost AI-assisted flood detection tools, the Centre for Strategic and International Studies describes a growing Pacific power vacuum, and the OECD Progress Report 2026 notes declining monitoring frameworks across 62% of partner countries.

This analysis adds a specific, quantified dimension: the moment the withdrawal became visible in the only cross-funder financial dataset that covers the Pacific, tracked day by day across 14 countries.

The finding connects to the IATI publication cadence analysis: only 35% of Pacific aid comes from funders with current data. The US withdrawal removes one of the few agencies that was publishing until recently, further narrowing the visible picture.

Methodology

This analysis draws on 41 consecutive daily snapshots of IATI data for 14 Pacific island countries, collected by the Pacific Aid Signal from 2026-09-08 to 2026-10-22. Each snapshot queries the IATI Datastore for all transactions tagged to these countries, weighted by declared recipient-country share (excluding approximately 70% of tagged transactions that are for other countries).

The 90-day trailing window captures disbursements with value dates in the prior 90 days from each snapshot date. “Lifetime” figures include all transactions on record regardless of date. US agencies are identified by IATI publisher reference prefix “US-”.
